Financial Recording

Day-to-day bookkeeping — sales, purchases, expenses, journals and bank entries recorded accurately in Tally, Zoho or your existing system.

Overview

What Financial Recording involves

This is the foundation everything else sits on. Every invoice, payment and expense recorded in the right ledger, under the right head, in the right period — so that when a return is filed or an auditor asks a question, the answer is already in the books.

Typical timeline

Ongoing entry through the month, with the period closed within 7 working days of month end.

Who it's for

  • Startups without an accountant
  • Small businesses
  • Professional practices
  • Businesses with a bookkeeping backlog
In Detail

Exactly what is covered

Every part of the work, spelled out — so you know what you are paying for before you commit.

01

Sales and purchase entries

Invoice-wise recording against the correct party ledgers, with GST treatment applied at entry rather than corrected later.

02

Expense vouchers

Expenses booked under a consistent chart of accounts, so month-on-month comparisons actually mean something.

03

Bank and cash books

All bank and cash movements recorded and tied back to statements, with no unexplained balances carried forward.

04

Journal entries

Provisions, prepayments, depreciation, accruals and adjustment entries passed with a documented basis.

05

Party ledgers

Customer and supplier accounts maintained so outstanding balances are accurate and disputes are traceable to an invoice.

06

Chart of accounts setup

If you are starting fresh, a structure suited to your business rather than a generic default you outgrow in a year.

Avoid These

Common mistakes we prevent

The errors that quietly cost the most — and what we do differently.

01

A catch-all expense ledger

'Miscellaneous expenses' destroys month-on-month comparability and is precisely what an assessing officer asks about.

We check for this
02

Mixing personal and business spending

It inflates expenses, weakens the books at audit, and makes the owner's drawings impossible to track.

We check for this
03

Entering without the GST treatment

Recording an invoice without its tax treatment means the books and the return disagree from the very start.

We check for this
04

Recording GST-inclusive amounts as revenue

It inflates turnover and understates the tax payable, and it is one of the first things a reviewer tests.

We check for this
